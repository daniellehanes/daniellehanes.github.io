Product Case Study · A tool I'm building for myself

This side project started with me, Mellie, and a notebook I never opened.

Outside of my product work, I train and compete in dog sports with my dog, Mellie. Like most handlers, I ran our training on memory and gut feel — scribbled notes, mental checklists, and a vague sense of whether we were actually improving.

It's the app I wished existed: the one I'd genuinely use to plan our sessions, capture what happened, see our progress, and walk into a trial prepared. I'm my own first user — so I started by designing for the person I know best.

The problem — from my own experience

Our training was constant. My visibility into it wasn't.

Training Mellie for competition means months of short, frequent sessions across skills that build toward a title. But almost none of that work lived anywhere durable for me. It was in my head, in a notebook I rarely reopened, or in scattered notes. I couldn't easily see our progress, I couldn't diagnose plateaus, and the jump from our practice yard to the trial ring lost a lot in translation.

So I'd repeat what was comfortable instead of what we actually needed, discover gaps only on trial day, and struggle to tell whether a rough run was a training problem, a proofing problem, or just nerves. I kept thinking: there should be a better way to do this — so I decided to design it.

What I want to build: a low-friction, discipline-flexible app that lets a handler plan sessions, log reps in seconds, see patterns over time, and turn months of training into a confident trial-day routine — starting with the exact tool I'd use with Mellie.

Product principles

Five principles I'm using to keep the product honest as it takes shape:

01

Log in seconds, not minutes

Capture happens ring-side, one-handed, often with Mellie on leash. If logging isn't near-instant, it won't happen.

02

Show progress, not just data

I don't want a spreadsheet. I want to know what's improving, what's stuck, and what to work on next.

03

Discipline-flexible by default

Agility, obedience, rally, scent work — the core loop stays the same while the skill library adapts to the sport.

04

Built for the training-to-trial arc

The product's job isn't logging for its own sake — it's helping us perform when it counts.

05

Reinforcement, not judgment

Good training is positive. The tone celebrates consistency and effort rather than scolding missed days.

Users & research

Designing from empathy — starting with myself

Even building for myself, I ran a lightweight design-thinking process rather than jumping to features. I empathy-mapped my own experience with Mellie first, then pressure-tested it against other handlers I train and compete with — so the product reflects real needs, not just my assumptions.
